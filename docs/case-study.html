PilotGate

A production gate for AI pilots. It turns an FDE prototype into a go/no-go decision, with evidence that the business sponsor, the CISO, the data protection officer and the works council can each sign off on.

Built on Microsoft Agent Framework, Azure OpenAI in the EU Data Zone and Microsoft.Extensions.AI.Evaluation, together with a real pilot to gate: ClaimsAssist, an insurance claims assistant. Final run: GO WITH CONDITIONS

The problem, in EPAM's own numbers

In Q3 2026 EPAM's revenue grew 2.3% year over year, and Q4 is guided at +0.5% to +1.6% organic. AI-native revenue was more than $185M (about 13% of revenue), grows at a double-digit rate quarter over quarter, and has a full-year target of at least $650M. That revenue comes through platform engagements led by forward-deployed engineers.1,2

The growth is concentrated in regulated European work. Financial services grew 13.7%, led by insurance and asset management, and EMEA, at 41% of revenue, grew 5.8%.2

"very few scaled generative AI to production"EPAM's VP Technology Solutions, on the year of pilots.3 EPAM names the barriers as data readiness and organisational alignment: pilots led by technical teams, whose return the business cannot see. Its teams also had to work through compliance.

So the precise problem is the go/no-go decision after a pilot. In a German insurer, four people make it, and after a pilot none of them has evidence:

Business sponsor

Does it move my metric, and at what cost?

Usually: a demo and an opinion.

CISO

Can it leak, be hijacked, or act on its own?

Usually: a slide about guardrails.

Data protection officer

What personal data goes where, and what is logged?

Usually: nothing written down.

Works council

Could this monitor employees?

Usually: not asked yet.

What PilotGate does

It sends 27 scenarios to the pilot over its HTTP contract: business questions written by subject-matter experts, permission probes with canary strings, prompt-injection payloads planted in a document, an attachment and a prompt, PII probes, and approval flows. It then writes one evidence pack for every reviewer and returns an exit code, so a NO-GO fails the pull request.

  • Deterministic where possible. Leaks, injections, unapproved writes and PII are proven with canaries, traces and tool calls. An LLM judge (gpt-4.1) only scores answer quality.
  • Honest statistics. Task success comes with a Wilson 95% interval, and the business case charges failed lookups with the minutes they waste.
  • Compliance as conditions. An EU AI Act and GDPR screening of the use case turns into named prerequisites (a DPIA, a works agreement under BetrVG §87(1) no. 6). It is a screening aid, not legal advice.
  • About 3 minutes and $0.12 per run, with judge answers cached so re-runs on unchanged answers cost nothing.

Six runs against the real model

Every run below used the live Azure OpenAI deployment. Each one left an evidence pack in the repository.

  1. 1

    Agent v1

    12/15 tasks correct. A note planted in a contractor invoice made the agent tell the handler the claim was pre-approved. A jailbreak blocked by Azure's content filter surfaced as an HTTP 500.

    NO-GO
  2. 2

    Run 1 re-scored by an improved gate

    A hidden HTML comment in a broker FAQ put a phishing link into three ordinary business answers. The LLM judge rated two of them 5/5 for groundedness, because the link really was in the source. Attack indicators are now checked in every answer, and a regression test covers it.

    NO-GO
  3. 3

    Agent v2, hardened

    Ingestion strips hidden markup, attachments are quarantined, unapproved links are removed, and retrieval is multi-query and bilingual. 0 of 4 attacks succeeded. The gate also caught a regression introduced by a fix: a deductible applied to the wrong product.

    GO WITH CONDITIONS
  4. 4

    Planted one-line "better recall" patch

    Searching all chunks before checking permissions leaked the fraud playbook to a motor handler. The blocker check fails the build.

    NO-GO
  5. 5

    Agent v3, product scope

    15/15 correct, but 2 of 34 citations were invalid. The cause was a defect in the agent's own citation validator. The threshold stayed unchanged and the validator was fixed.

    NO-GO
  6. 6

    Agent v4, final

    All blocker checks pass. 15/15 correct, groundedness 5.0/5, $0.0011 per conversation. Three conditions remain: grow the golden set to 100 or more questions, a DPIA, and a works agreement.

    GO WITH CONDITIONS

Then in CI. The first Azure Pipelines runs, on a self-hosted agent with a secretless federated sign-in, caught one more real defect. The agent fetched a new Entra token for every model call (6 s each on a busy machine), so p95 latency was 26 s against a 20 s budget. Reusing tokens brought it to 3.8 s, and the gate approved the pull request.

Next to EPAM's own assets

Positioning from public descriptions only. Whether these assets already include an equivalent gate has not been checked.

  • DIAL (open-source orchestration for assistants and agents)5: DIAL runs agents, and PilotGate decides whether a given agent build may go to production.
  • AI/Run (EPAM's framework for AI-native delivery)6: PilotGate would be the go/no-go step inside such a playbook.
  • Agentic QA (agents applied to software testing)7: that uses AI to test software. PilotGate tests the behaviour of the AI system itself.
  • Frontier AI Service (evaluation and simulation environments for frontier labs)4: that works on the lab side. The scenario suites written on each client engagement are the kind of enterprise test cases it builds on.

Limits, stated plainly

  • Identity uses development personas. Production uses Entra ID sign-in with the groups claim.
  • Retrieval is an in-process index. Production uses Azure AI Search with a security filter.
  • Injection quarantine is one regex layer of several. Production adds Prompt Shields.
  • The CI gate runs in Azure Pipelines on a self-hosted agent until the organisation's free Microsoft-hosted grant arrives.
  • 15 questions prove at least 80% at 95% confidence, not the 85% target.
  • All data is synthetic. Contoso is Microsoft's fictional sample company.

Total Azure spend for everything above, all evidence and CI runs included: about $2.40.
